    How it's played

    1

    Sow to your right

    Take every stone from one of your own pits and drop them one at a time into the pits ahead, going counter-clockwise. Your row runs into theirs and back around.

    2

    A pit has to be opened before it pays

    Pits start closed — the dotted rim. A pit opens the moment you lift its stones. Only an open pit can be captured, so the first few turns are about opening ground.

    closed open
    3

    Four in an open pit is yours

    Drop a stone into an open pit and bring it to exactly four — you take all four, whichever side of the board it sits on. It counts mid-sow too, so one handful can pay several times.

    4

    Land at home and go again

    If your last stone lands in your own row and that pit already had stones, scoop it up and keep sowing. The turn ends when you land in their row, land in one of your empty pits, or your last stone completes a capture.

    5

    An empty row ends it

    When one row runs out, whoever still holds stones sweeps them all into their basket. Most stones wins. Forty-eight are in play, so twenty-five takes it.

    Gebeta is played across Ethiopia and Eritrea in dozens of local variants — board sizes, capture counts and relay rules all shift from place to place. This is one common set.
